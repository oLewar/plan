# Margin and constraint (which knob actually moved)

## Definition (working)

A choice is a **constraint** plus a **margin**. The constraint is what the agent cannot have both of (income, time, a capacity, a patent term). The margin is the rate at which they trade one thing for another (the slope: marginal rate of substitution, marginal cost, a wage against an hour). Most policy arguments name an outcome and skip both.

Working source: [[wiki/sources/intermediate-microeconomics-emerson]]. The book does not invent the split. It drills it as the spine of 24 modules, each opened by a policy question and closed by putting that question back on the same diagram.

```
outcome people argue about
        │
        ├─ preferences / tastes        → indifference, utility (modules 1–2)
        ├─ what they can afford        → budget (module 3)
        ├─ the tradeoff at the chosen point → MRS = price ratio (module 4)
        ├─ the firm's cost of one more unit → marginal cost (modules 7–9)
        └─ a side effect not in the price  → externality (module 20)
                 └─ naming the wrong row is how a policy "doesn't work"
```

## Why it matters for `pro/plan`

- Same shape as [[wiki/concepts/causal-analysis]]: "the policy failed" is not one cause. A hybrid-car tax credit can fail because people didn't want the car (preferences), because the credit didn't change the budget enough, or because the fuel they stopped buying was replaced by something else. Modules 1–4 are that one question seen four ways.
- Efficiency ([[wiki/concepts/efficiency-metric]]): pushing the knob that isn't binding is pure cost. A minimum-wage fight that never looks at the firm's cost minimization (module 7) is that mistake. A carbon-tax fight that never looks at the supply curve (module 9) is the same mistake on the other side.
- Deadweight loss, in this book, is a heading in module 10: the surplus that the constraint destroyed, not a synonym for "bad". Don't use the word looser than that.
- Timeless pole of [[wiki/concepts/barbell-strategy]]. The 2005–2012 hooks (hybrid credits, Crestor, the individual mandate) will date. The split will not. Read the object, not the headline.
- Not a claim that the book's answers to those hooks are right. The questions are motivation. The modules were not read through here, and module 19 is an outline (~1,300 words).
